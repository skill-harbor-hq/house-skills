# Fêtes et dates 2026-2027 — les 8 sabbats, solstices et équinoxes

The skill's only source of dates. Two kinds of date live here, and they are never confused:

- **Astronomical dates** (solstices and equinoxes): verifiable facts. Exact day and time are given in UTC first, then in America/Toronto time for convenience. UTC is the reference if the two ever seem to disagree.
- **Traditional dates** (the eight festivals): calendar dates from pagan, Wiccan, and folkloric tradition. Four festivals sit on or near an astronomical moment (Ostara, Litha, Mabon, Yule); four sit at traditional fixed dates between them (Imbolc, Beltane, Lughnasadh, Samhain). A traditional date is not an astronomical moment, and some traditions shift it by a day.

Meanings and practices below are summaries of what those traditions commonly associate with each festival, written in our own words. They are labelled tradition throughout, and the user's own practice overrides them.

## Verification of the astronomical dates (2 sources, cross-checked 2026-10-10)

The difference is a rounding / ephemeris-version difference of one minute, not a date disagreement. ## The eight festivals (northern hemisphere order, starting at Imbolc)

### 1. Imbolc — around February 1
- **Traditional date:** February 1 (some traditions February 2). Fixed traditional date, not an astronomical moment.
- **Traditional meaning (tradition):** the first stirrings under late winter: lengthening light, seeds and plans still underground, purification and preparation for spring. Associated in the tradition with Brigid, fire, and milk and early lambs in older farming folklore.
- **Traditional practices:** lighting candles, cleaning and clearing a space, setting a quiet intention for spring, making or blessing seeds and tools, a simple dairy or grain-based meal.

### 2. Ostara — March equinox
- **Astronomical date:** March equinox, see table above (2026: March 20; 2027: March 20).
- **Traditional meaning (tradition):** balance of day and night, then light gaining. Renewal, planting, beginnings that can now be acted on rather than only planned.
- **Traditional practices:** planting seeds, eggs and early flowers as seasonal symbols, a spring walk, opening windows and airing the house, starting the project prepared at Imbolc.

### 3. Beltane — around May 1
- **Traditional date:** May 1 (celebrations traditionally beginning the evening before). Fixed traditional date, not an astronomical moment.
- **Traditional meaning (tradition):** full spring, fertility of land and life in the broad folkloric sense, fire, joy, and union and creativity as traditional themes.
- **Traditional practices:** outdoor fire or candles where safe and allowed, flowers, dancing or music in folkloric retellings, a festive shared meal, time in green places.

### 4. Litha — June solstice
- **Astronomical date:** June solstice, see table above (2026: June 21; 2027: June 21).
- **Traditional meaning (tradition):** the longest day and the peak of light, then the slow turn back. Abundance of the growing season, strength, and gratitude for light, stated as tradition, never as a promise.
- **Traditional practices:** watching sunrise or sunset, herbs and flowers gathered in season, an outdoor meal, noting what is growing well in one's own life or garden, in one's own words.

### 5. Lughnasadh — around August 1
- **Traditional date:** August 1 (also spelled Lughnasa; also called Lammas in some traditions). Fixed traditional date, not an astronomical moment.
- **Traditional meaning (tradition):** first harvest, especially grain. Skill, work, and the first results of what was planted in spring; also, traditionally, games and gatherings.
- **Traditional practices:** baking bread, sharing a harvest meal, recognising one's own work and skills, local fairs or games in the folkloric version, preserving food.

### 6. Mabon — September equinox
- **Astronomical date:** September equinox, see table above (2026: September 23 UTC / September 22 local evening in Toronto; 2027: September 23).
- **Traditional meaning (tradition):** second harvest, balance again, day and night equal before darkness gains. Thanksgiving for the harvest in the traditional sense, and taking stock.
- **Traditional practices:** a harvest meal (apples, squash, grain), balancing accounts and plans in a practical sense, preserving and storing, a gratitude note in the journal, in the user's words.

### 7. Samhain — around October 31 / November 1
- **Traditional date:** October 31 into November 1 (celebrations traditionally beginning the evening of October 31). Fixed traditional date, not an astronomical moment.
- **Traditional meaning (tradition):** end of the harvest season and start of the dark half of the year in the tradition; remembrance of the dead and of ancestors, and a traditional sense of a thin boundary, presented strictly as tradition and folklore, never as a factual claim about spirits or the future.
- **Traditional practices:** remembering people who have died, photos or names at a meal, an extra place set in some folkloric versions, candles, quiet, closing the outdoor season, divination in some traditions (the skill offers it only if the user chose it, and only as tradition).

### 8. Yule — December solstice
- **Astronomical date:** December solstice, see table above (2026: December 21; 2027: December 22 UTC / December 21 local evening in Toronto).
- **Traditional meaning (tradition):** the longest night and the return of the light after it. Rest, endurance, hope stated as a traditional symbol, and the start of a new wheel.
- **Traditional practices:** evergreens, candles or a Yule log in folkloric versions, a warm shared meal, rest, gifts in some family traditions, setting a word or intention for the new wheel, in the user's words.

## Southern hemisphere shift

Shift every festival by six months: Samhain around May 1, Yule at the June solstice, Imbolc around August 1, Ostara at the September equinox, Beltane around November 1, Litha at the December solstice, Lughnasadh around February 1, Mabon at the March equinox. The astronomical moments are the same instants worldwide; only their seasonal name flips.

## Quick next-festival logic (for session checks)

Order within a northern calendar year: Imbolc (Feb 1), Ostara (Mar equinox), Beltane (May 1), Litha (Jun solstice), Lughnasadh (Aug 1), Mabon (Sep equinox), Samhain (Oct 31), Yule (Dec solstice), then Imbolc of the next year. The next festival on or after a given date is the first entry in that order whose date is on or after it. Example used in testing: on 2026-10-10, the next festival is Samhain, October 31, 2026.
