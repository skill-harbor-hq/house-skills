# Recall hygiene and the traditional symbol dictionary

Two different kinds of content live in this file, and the skill never mixes them: **recall hygiene** is mainstream practice advice (labelled as such), and the **symbol dictionary** is tradition — what published dream dictionaries associate, not what a symbol means for this user. The user's own journal history outranks the dictionary every time: if their dreams of water are always joyful, the dictionary's "emotions" line is trivia, not a reading.

## Recall hygiene (practice, not medicine)

- **Capture within minutes.** Dream memory decays extremely fast after waking; the standard advice is to record before getting up, even as fragments or keywords.
- **Intention before sleep.** Deciding "I will remember my dreams" is the most-cited recall lever, and journaling itself trains recall over the first one to two weeks.
- **Log the blanks.** A "no recall" entry keeps the habit and the honest denominator.
- **Regular hours help**; long, lighter sleep tends to be remembered better than short, crushed sleep. Abrupt alarms and alcohol in the evening are commonly reported recall killers — worth tracking as context if the user wants correlations, never lectured about.
- **Don't force a narrative.** Record fragments as fragments; a story invented to fill gaps contaminates the journal's patterns.
- **Reviewing helps.** Re-reading recent entries before sleep is a common practice step for both recall and lucid work.

None of this is medical advice. Sleep disorders, sleep apnea signs, or persistent distressing nightmares belong with a professional; the skill may say so once, gently (see the honesty guard).

## Traditional symbol dictionary (tradition — always labelled)

What classic dream dictionaries most often associate with common images. Offer at most 2-3 relevant lines, introduced as "the dictionaries traditionally say…", and stop there.

| Image | Traditional association |
|---|---|
| Flying | Freedom, escape, ambition; in lucid practice, a classic lucidity trigger |
| Falling | Loss of control, insecurity, a change not chosen |
| Teeth (losing, crumbling) | Anxiety about appearance or being understood; powerlessness in some traditions |
| Water (sea, river, flood) | Emotions and their state: calm, murky, rising |
| Being chased | Avoidance — something in waking life not faced |
| Being late / missing transport | Fear of missing an opportunity or a deadline pressure |
| Houses and rooms | The self and its parts; a new room, a new aspect discovered |
| Stairs | Transition between states, effort, rising or descending fortunes |
| Snakes | Transformation in many traditions; threat or fear in others — genuinely divided |
| Death (of someone living) | Endings and change in the relationship, classically — not a literal omen in the dictionary tradition itself |
| Exams / back at school | Being judged, unpreparedness, an old standard re-applied |
| Vehicles | Direction and control over one's course; who is driving matters in the tradition |
| Mirrors | Self-image, seeing oneself as others do |
| Doors (locked, opening) | Opportunities, thresholds, the unknown on the other side |

## Lucid dreaming — the practice in brief (for the module)

- **Dream signs** are the royal road in the practice literature: recurring elements are the moments lucidity most often sparks. This skill's reviews extract them from the user's real journal instead of generic lists.
- **Reality checks**: brief daytime habits of questioning one's state (looking at hands, re-reading a line of text, pinching the nose and trying to breathe). The habit is meant to carry into dreams.
- **MILD-style intention**: before sleep, recalling the last dream and imagining noticing its dream sign. The skill can build the sentence from the user's own top sign.
- **WBTB** (wake back to bed) exists in the literature; the skill mentions it only if the user asks, always with the sleep-first guardrail: it fragments sleep and is optional, never a regimen.
- No technique is promised to work; the log decides what works **for this user**.
